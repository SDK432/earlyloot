Rockstar almost never talks about how its games get made, but on October 14, 2026 it opened the doors for [Bloomberg Businessweek](https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2026-grand-theft-auto-6-making-of/). Jason Schreier's feature, reported with Rockstar's cooperation, explains why **Grand Theft Auto VI** took more than eight years: a far bigger original plan, years of AI work, pandemic setbacks and a cast of NPCs that forced a delay. The release date hasn't moved — still **November 19, 2026** on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S.

## It started as "Project Americas"

The long-rumored codename is real. According to [Polygon's summary](https://www.polygon.com/gta-6-bloomberg-jason-schreier/), Bloomberg reports the game was called **Project Americas** because it aimed to cover large areas of both North and South America. That scope was later narrowed to Miami and its surroundings — the Vice City and Leonida we know now. [Polygon](https://www.polygon.com/gta-6-bloomberg-jason-rockstar-development/) says the overly ambitious start helps explain the delays.

Production also got stuck behind *Red Dead Redemption 2*. Rockstar planned to build both games side by side, but co-studio head **Rob Nelson** had to pull most of the company onto RDR2, pushing GTA VI back several years, [Polygon reports](https://www.polygon.com/gta-6-bloomberg-jason-schreier/). Nelson called one stretch "a dark period."

## Realism, AI and remote work

Nelson told Bloomberg the team struggled to define how much realism players would expect, using the example of simply drinking a beer, per [Polygon](https://www.polygon.com/gta-6-bloomberg-jason-rockstar-development/). Making Jason and Lucia work as a true couple meant building complex AI systems — "the traditional kind, not ChatGPT," as Schreier put it — which took years.

The pandemic hurt too. [Polygon](https://www.polygon.com/gta-6-bloomberg-jason-rockstar-development/) notes that performance capture needs people in the same room, and staff working from home weren't allowed to access Rockstar's servers and tools because of privacy concerns.

## An NPC studio — and a delay

After the 2023 reveal, leadership realized the game's NPCs would need far more time and manpower than planned. Rockstar built a studio dedicated to non-playable characters, and [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/games/1011028/next-gta-faster-development) says that contributed to the delay. Head of development **Phil Hooker** put it bluntly: "We could hit that schedule, but it won't be the game we want to make." That fits what we heard earlier about Rockstar's [dedicated pedestrian dialogue team](/articles/gta-6-pedestrian-dialogue-writers/).

## Budget and headcount

Rockstar shared no budget figure. Per [Polygon](https://www.polygon.com/gta-6-bloomberg-jason-schreier/), head of publishing **Jennifer Kolbe** "did shoot down a popular rumor that it surpassed the $1.5 billion it cost to build Dubai's Burj Khalifa." Schreier still argues it's likely the most expensive game ever made. Rockstar has grown from **2,441** employees around RDR2 to **4,711** across 12 offices, [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/rockstar-says-you-wont-have-to-wait-another-8-years-after-gta-6-for-its-next-game/) notes.

## The next game should be faster

Hooker told Bloomberg that "the engine, the tools, and the team we've built during this project will allow us to do things more quickly," [GameSpot reports](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/rockstar-says-you-wont-have-to-wait-another-8-years-after-gta-6-for-its-next-game/). [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/games/1011028/next-gta-faster-development) points to *GTA III* and *Vice City*, released about a year apart. No new game has been announced.

## Two new screenshots

The feature also came with two new official stills, [Rockstar Report notes](https://rockstarreport.com/news/new-gta-6-screenshots-muddy-racing-ocean-drive): Jason and Lucia covered in mud at an off-road gathering, and a police helicopter chasing a green supercar on Ocean Drive.
